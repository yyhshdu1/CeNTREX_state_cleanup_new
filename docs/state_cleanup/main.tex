\chapter{Rotational-state cleanup}
\label{ch:cleanup}

After the electrostatic quadrupole lens (EQL), the molecules in $J=2$, $m_J=0$ are focused and
dominate the population. The state-preparation stage B (SPB) transfers the singlet state
$|J=2,F_1=\tfrac52,F=2,m_F=0\rangle$ to $J=1$, the science state used in the interaction
region~\cite{Grasdijk2021}. Two cleanup stages are placed around this transfer. CU1, before SPB,
removes the molecules that are already in $J=1$, which would otherwise reach the interaction region
together with the transferred ones. CU2, after SPB, removes the population left in $J=2$, which the
state-preparation stage C (SPC) would otherwise transfer to $J=1$. Both stages pump the molecules
optically through the $B\,{}^3\Pi_1$ state into other rotational levels, in the applied electric
field. This chapter first describes the pumping schemes and the simulation model
(Secs.~\ref{sec:pumping} and~\ref{sec:model}), and then the simulations and test results of each
stage (Secs.~\ref{sec:cu1} and~\ref{sec:cu2}). The cleanup chambers and field electrodes were
designed by Jianhui Li, and we assembled them together. All
simulations are built on the \code{centrex-tlf} package~\cite{centrextlf}, written by Olivier
Grasdijk. The rest of the work described here is my own: the design of the
CU1 and CU2 pumping schemes, the cleanup simulations and the optimisation of the set points, and the
tests on the SPB setup, from taking the data to the analysis.

\section{Optical pumping in the electric field}
\label{sec:pumping}

Each cleanup stage drives a transition from the addressed rotational level $J$ of $X$ to $B$. Once
excited, a molecule decays spontaneously to several rotational levels, and most decays end in a level
that the light does not address: for CU1 only \SI{37}{\percent} of the decays return to $J=1$, and for
CU2 only \SI{27}{\percent} return to $J=2$ (Table~\ref{tab:stages}). A molecule is therefore removed
from its original rotational level after emitting one or two photons.
% NOTE: delete the following parenthesis if the notation is defined in the previous chapters
(States are labelled $|J,F_1,F,m_F,P\rangle$, with the nuclear spins coupled as
$\mathbf J+\mathbf I_1=\mathbf F_1$ and $\mathbf F_1+\mathbf I_2=\mathbf F$; primed quantum numbers
refer to $B$, and R($J$) denotes $J\to J'=J+1$.) The $J=1$ level has 12 sublevels and $J=2$ has 20.

The electric fields, \SI{150}{V/cm} for CU1 and \SI{217}{V/cm} for CU2, define the quantisation
axis $z$. In the field, $m_F$ remains a good quantum number, while $J$, $F_1$, $F$ and the parity of
$B$ are mixed. The hyperfine structure of each ground $J$ level spans only about \SI{2}{MHz}, but in
the excited $J'$ levels the Stark interaction separates neighbouring $|m_F'|$ sublevels by
10--\SI{25}{MHz}, which is many natural linewidths ($\Gamma/2\pi=\SI{1.61}{MHz}$). A single laser
frequency can then reach only a few excited sublevels. For a fixed polarization, $N_g$ ground
sublevels coupled to $N_e$ excited ones leave at least $N_g-N_e$ dark states. Both schemes use three
tools to solve this problem:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{use the field to mix the ground states}, which makes zero-field-forbidden transitions
        driveable. For example, $|J=1,F_1=\tfrac12,F=0\rangle$ cannot be excited to $F'=2$ at zero
        field, but at \SI{150}{V/cm} it carries a \SI{29}{\percent} admixture of
        $|F_1=\tfrac32,F=2\rangle$, through which CU1 pumps it.
  \item \textbf{reach more excited sublevels}, with the carrier placed between two excited states,
        with phase-modulation sidebands, or with a second beam on a second hyperfine level.
  \item \textbf{switch the polarization} between X ($\perp\mathbf E$) and Z ($\parallel\mathbf E$)
        at \SI{0.5}{MHz} to mix bright and dark states.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

In the model, a molecule will cross the region with electric field and pump laser, and the final state will be read out in the end. Its
internal state is described by a density matrix $\rho$, which evolves under the Lindblad master
equation (optical Bloch equations, OBE) of the field-dressed $X$ and $B$ states, coupled by the
light and damped by spontaneous decay. The following subsections describe, in the order in which the
model is built, the states that are kept (Sec.~\ref{sec:model_states}), their coupling by the light
(Sec.~\ref{sec:model_coupling}), spontaneous decay (Sec.~\ref{sec:model_decay}), the light seen by
the molecule along its trajectory (Sec.~\ref{sec:model_laser}), the initial states and observables
(Sec.~\ref{sec:model_obs}) and the integration (Sec.~\ref{sec:model_num}). CU1 and CU2 are built by
the same code, from \code{centrex-tlf}~\cite{centrextlf}, and differ only in the parameters of
Table~\ref{tab:stages}. The assumptions are collected in Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}.

\subsection{States}
\label{sec:model_states}

\paragraph{Hamiltonians and fields.} The $X$ and $B$ Hamiltonians include the rotation (for $X$ with
the quartic centrifugal-distortion term), the magnetic hyperfine and nuclear spin--rotation
interactions of the two nuclei, the $\Lambda$-doubling of $B$, and the Stark and Zeeman interactions.
They are diagonalised in the applied field, $\mathbf E=E\hat{\mathbf z}$ with $z$ vertical, over
$J=0\ldots J'+2$ for $X$ and $J'=1\ldots J'+2$ for $B$, where $J'$ is the addressed excited level
($J'=2$ for CU1, 3 for CU2), each in the full hyperfine basis and, for $B$, with both parities. At
these fields a larger basis changes the energies by less than \SI{0.1}{kHz}. A magnetic field of
\SI{1e-3}{\gauss} along $z$ is added. It has no effect on the cleanup, but it lifts the degeneracy of
the $\pm m_F$ pairs. Without it, the two eigenvectors of a degenerate pair are an arbitrary mixture
of $+m_F$ and $-m_F$ that depends on the linear-algebra library, and X-polarised light, which couples
to both members of a pair, would see an ill-defined coupling.

\paragraph{What the field does to the states.} In the field only $m_F$, the projection of the total
angular momentum on $\mathbf E$, remains a good quantum number. The other quantum numbers are mixed,
but very differently in $X$ and in $B$.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item In $X$, neighbouring rotational levels are tens of GHz apart, so $J$ is mixed only weakly. The
        field mainly mixes the hyperfine levels $(F_1,F)$ within one $J$; all sublevels of one $J$
        lie within \SI{2.4}{MHz} ($J=1$ at \SI{150}{V/cm}) or \SI{1.7}{MHz} ($J=2$ at
        \SI{217}{V/cm}). The ground sublevels are therefore
        superpositions of the zero-field hyperfine states; for example,
        $|J=1,F_1=\tfrac12,F=0\rangle$ carries \SI{29}{\percent} of $|F_1=\tfrac32,F=2\rangle$ at
        \SI{150}{V/cm}. These admixtures make transitions driveable that are forbidden at zero field
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:pumping}).
  \item In $B$ ($\Omega=\pm1$), every hyperfine level consists of two $\Lambda$-doublet components of
        opposite parity, only \SI{13.9}{MHz} apart for $J'=2$, $F_1'=\tfrac52$, $F'=2$ and
        \SI{5.1}{MHz} apart for $J'=3$, $F_1'=\tfrac72$, $F'=3$. The field couples the two
        components in first order, with a matrix element proportional to $m_F'$. In the cleanup
        fields this coupling is much larger than the $\Lambda$ splitting. At \SI{217}{V/cm} the two
        $|m_F'|=3$ sublevels of $J'=3$, $F'=3$ are \SI{93}{MHz} apart, and each is a 53:47 mixture of
        the two parities. Only $m_F'=0$ stays parity-pure (\SI{99.9}{\percent}), because the
        first-order term vanishes there. This is the linear Stark splitting of the $m_F'$ sublevels
        that the pumping schemes have to deal with (Sec.~\ref{sec:pumping}). In addition, the
        hyperfine interaction mixes neighbouring $J'$, already at zero field: the $J'=3$,
        $F_1'=\tfrac72$ levels contain \SI{7}{\percent} of $J'=4$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Labels.} Each eigenstate in the field is labelled by the zero-field basis state
$|J,F_1,F,m_F,P\rangle$ with which it has the largest overlap; primed numbers refer to $B$. With both
parities of $B$ mixed almost equally, this largest overlap is barely above one half (0.49--0.56 for
$|m_F'|\geq1$ at \SI{217}{V/cm}), so the assignment was checked against adiabatic following: the
field is raised from zero in steps of \SI{0.5}{V/cm}, and at every step each eigenvector is matched to
the one of the previous step with the largest overlap. At 150 and \SI{217}{V/cm} both methods give
the same label to all 280 $B$ states of $J'=1$--5. For $X$ the two methods agree up to about
\SI{10}{kV/cm}. A label such as $P'=-$ therefore names the zero-field parent of a state, not its
parity.

\paragraph{Kept states and compaction.} The diagonalised basis contains several hundred states, but
only a few interact with the light. The OBE system keeps
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item all sublevels of the addressed ground level (12 for $J=1$, 20 for $J=2$), with all
        coherences;
  \item all $m_F'$ sublevels of the addressed excited hyperfine level or levels;
  \item every other rotational level of $X$ that the excited states decay to, each
        \emph{compacted} into a single level.
\end{itemize}
Compaction is exact for the cleanup: no light acts on these levels, so only their total population,
fed by decay, matters. All other $B$ states are not dynamical levels; they enter only through the
mixing they produce in the kept states. The resulting models have 26 levels for CU1 and 56 for CU2
(Table~\ref{tab:stages}).

\paragraph{Opposite-parity levels.} The parity mixing of the kept $B$ states is always included,
through the diagonalisation. The question is only whether the partner sublevels of the other parity
parent must also be kept as dynamical levels, and that depends on whether the light reaches them.
For CU1 the partners of $J'=2$, $F'=2$, $P'=+$ lie above the driven sublevels; at the CU1 laser
frequency the nearest partners that can be excited are \SI{40}{MHz} ($25\,\Gamma$) away, and keeping
them changes the cleanup by only $6\times10^{-6}$. They are kept nevertheless, so that both stages
are built in the same way, and the CU1 model has 26 levels instead of 21. For CU2 the partners
of $F'=3$ and $F'=4$ lie inside the frequency range covered by the phase-modulation sidebands of the
two beams. At the set point of Sec.~\ref{sec:cu2_opt}, about \SI{30}{\percent} of the excitation by
the $F'=4$ beam goes to the $|m_F'|=1$ sublevels of the $P'=+$ parent, through the first upper
sideband (weak-field estimate with the Doppler width). All 32 excited sublevels of $F'=3$ and $F'=4$
are therefore kept.

\subsection{Coupling to the light}
\label{sec:model_coupling}

\paragraph{Coupling matrices.} The light is described in the laser frame, in which $z$ is along
$\mathbf E$ and $x$ is horizontal, along the molecular beam; the light propagates horizontally,
across the molecular beam. For each beam $k$ and each polarization $p\in\{X,Z\}$, with
$\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}_X=\hat{\mathbf x}\perp\mathbf E$ and
$\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}_Z=\hat{\mathbf z}\parallel\mathbf E$, the coupling matrix is
\begin{equation}
  M^{(p)}_{k,eg}=\frac{\langle e|\,\mathbf d\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}_p\,|g\rangle}{d_{XB}},
  \qquad d_{XB}=\SI{2.6675506e-30}{C\,m},
  \label{eq:M}
\end{equation}
between the field-dressed states $g$ of the addressed ground level and $e$ of the excited level of
beam $k$. Z light drives $\Delta m_F=0$ and X light $\Delta m_F=\pm1$. Whether a pair is coupled is
decided by the magnitude of this mixed-state matrix element, not by the zero-field selection rules.
At zero field the two agree exactly; in the field, the admixtures described above make nominally
forbidden pairs driveable, and the pumping schemes rely on this.

\paragraph{Main pair and normalisation.} Each beam has a main pair $(g_0,e_0)$
(Table~\ref{tab:stages}), which serves two purposes. First, the detuning $\delta_k$ of beam $k$ is
measured from the resonance of its main pair,
\begin{equation}
  \omega_k=\omega_{e_0g_0}(E)+\delta_k,
  \label{eq:detuning}
\end{equation}
so $\delta_k>0$ is a blue detuning. Because the main resonance itself moves with the field, a fixed
$\delta_k$ is a different laser frequency at a different field. Second, the coupling matrices are
divided by the main-pair element $m_k=M^{(X)}_{k,e_0g_0}$, so that the main pair is driven at
exactly the Rabi rate $\Omega_k$ and every other pair at $\Omega_kM_{eg}/m_k$. The conversion from
laser intensity to $\Omega_k$ uses the same $m_k$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rabi}), so the result does not
depend on which pair is chosen as the main pair, provided that it is a strong one: $|m_k|$ is 0.100
for CU1 and 0.095 and 0.224 for the two CU2 beams.

\paragraph{Hamiltonian.} In the rotating-wave approximation, with $\hbar=1$ and all energies in
angular frequency units, the Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
  H(t)=H_0-\sum_k\delta_k\Pi_k
  +\frac12\sum_k\Bigl[\Omega_k(t)\sum_{p\in\{X,Z\}}g_{p,k}(t)\,\frac{M^{(p)}_k}{m_k}+\mathrm{h.c.}\Bigr].
  \label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
$H_0$ holds the field-dressed energies of the kept levels in the rotating frame, in which the main
pair of beam 0 is degenerate at $\delta_0=0$; what remains are the hyperfine and Stark splittings.
$\Pi_k$ projects onto the excited states of beam $k$. $\Omega_k(t)$ is the Rabi rate of the main pair
along the trajectory, complex when the light is phase-modulated, and $g_{p,k}(t)\in\{0,1\}$ is the
polarization gate (Sec.~\ref{sec:model_laser}).

\paragraph{Matrix form.} As an illustration, take two ground sublevels, the main ground state $g_0$ and
another sublevel $g_1$, and two excited sublevels, the main excited state $e_0$ and another sublevel
$e_1$, of one beam. In the basis $(g_0,g_1,e_0,e_1)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} reads
\begin{equation}
  H(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
    0 & 0 & \tfrac12\Omega^*\mu_{00}^* & \tfrac12\Omega^*\mu_{10}^*\\[2pt]
    0 & \varepsilon_1 & \tfrac12\Omega^*\mu_{01}^* & \tfrac12\Omega^*\mu_{11}^*\\[2pt]
    \tfrac12\Omega\,\mu_{00} & \tfrac12\Omega\,\mu_{01} & -\delta & 0\\[2pt]
    \tfrac12\Omega\,\mu_{10} & \tfrac12\Omega\,\mu_{11} & 0 & \Delta_1-\delta
  \end{pmatrix},\qquad
  \mu_{ji}(t)=\sum_{p\in\{X,Z\}}g_p(t)\,\frac{M^{(p)}_{e_jg_i}}{m}.
  \label{eq:Hmatrix}
\end{equation}
The upper-left block holds the ground energies from $g_0$ ($\varepsilon_1$ is at most \SI{2.4}{MHz}
in CU1), the lower-right block the excited energies from the main resonance, shifted by the detuning
($\Delta_1$ is a Stark splitting of tens of MHz). The off-diagonal blocks are the light: the main pair
has $\mu_{00}=1$ when its polarization is on and is driven at $\Omega$, every other pair at
$\Omega\mu_{ji}$. An entry vanishes when the polarization that is on cannot connect the two $m_F$
values, so the pattern changes at every switch. With $g_0$ and $e_0$ alone, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hmatrix} is
the two-level Hamiltonian
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\Omega^*/2\\\Omega/2&-\delta\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, resonant at
$\delta=0$; a pair $(g_i,e_j)$ is resonant at $\delta=\Delta_j-\varepsilon_i$. In CU1, for example,
the $|m_F'|=1$ sublevels of the $P'=+$ parent ($\Delta/2\pi=\SI{18.5}{MHz}$) and the SPB-related levels
($\varepsilon/2\pi\approx-\SI{2.3}{MHz}$) are resonant at $\delta/2\pi\approx\SI{20.8}{MHz}$. The full
matrices have 12 ground and 10 excited sublevels for CU1 and 20 and 32 for CU2; the compacted levels
add only diagonal entries.

For CU2 the two beams address two hyperfine levels
of the same $J'$, and all of $B$, $J'=3$ is put in the rotating frame of beam 0. $\Pi_0$ then
projects onto all 32 excited sublevels, there is no separate $\delta_1\Pi_1$ term, and the couplings
of beam 1 carry the phase factor $e^{\mathrm i(\omega_1-\omega_0)t}=e^{2\pi\mathrm i\fAOM t}$. The
detuning $\delta_1$ enters only through this phase, via Eq.~\eqref{eq:aom}. The change of frame is
exact, but the integrator has to resolve the \SI{5}{ns} period of the phase factor. Each beam couples
only to its own excited level. The neglected couplings of
beam 0 to $F'=4$ and of beam 1 to $F'=3$ are detuned by more than \SI{77}{MHz} ($48\,\Gamma$) at
the set point, including the third-order sidebands, and by more than \SI{45}{MHz} anywhere in the
scanned range.

\subsection{Spontaneous decay}
\label{sec:model_decay}

Every excited state decays at the rate $\Gamma=1/\tau=\SI{1.01e7}{s^{-1}}$
($\Gamma/2\pi=\SI{1.61}{MHz}$, $\tau=\SI{99}{ns}$). The branching ratios $b_{e\to g}$ are computed
from the dipole matrix elements between the field-dressed states, to every $X$ state of the
diagonalised basis, and normalised to one; decays into a compacted level are summed into that
level. The field-dressed branching is what makes the removal efficient. For CU2, the $P'=-$ parent
of $J'=3$ decays at zero field only to $J=2$ and $J=4$. At \SI{217}{V/cm} the $|m_F'|\geq1$
sublevels of $F'=3$ and $F'=4$ decay to
\begin{equation*}
  J=2:\ 0.21\text{--}0.32,\quad J=3:\ 0.38\text{--}0.59,\quad J=4:\ 0.18\text{--}0.29,\quad
  J=5:\ 0.012\text{--}0.019,
\end{equation*}
depending on the sublevel. The decays to $J=3$ come from the opposite-parity admixture, and those to
$J=5$ from its $J'=4$ part; the parity-pure $m_F'=0$ sublevels decay only to $J=2$ and $J=4$ (0.53
and 0.47). Averaged over the driven sublevels, \SI{27}{\percent} of the CU2 decays and
\SI{37}{\percent} of the CU1 decays return to the addressed level (Table~\ref{tab:stages}). With one
collapse operator per pair, the master equation is
\begin{equation}
  \dot\rho=-\mathrm i[H,\rho]+\sum_{e,g}\Bigl(C_{eg}\rho C_{eg}^\dagger-\tfrac12\{C_{eg}^\dagger
  C_{eg},\rho\}\Bigr),\qquad C_{eg}=\sqrt{\Gamma b_{e\to g}}\,|g\rangle\langle e|.
  \label{eq:lindblad}
\end{equation}
Two simplifications follow from this form.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item Decay transfers populations, but no coherences between ground sublevels; that would require one
        collapse operator per emitted polarization component. The cleanup depends on total
        populations, so the effect is expected to be small, but it is not zero where dark
        superpositions matter.
  \item Decay to the excited vibrational levels $X(v\geq1)$ is not modelled. A molecule that decays
        there is lost from the addressed level, while the model returns part of that decay to it, so
        the neglect can only underestimate the cleanup.
\end{itemize}
Every rotational level that the kept states decay to is in the model, so the system is closed:
$\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho=1$ to $10^{-14}$ along every trajectory.

\subsection{Laser field along the trajectory}
\label{sec:model_laser}

The molecule sees the light change in time as it crosses the multipass. Four time dependences enter
$\Omega_k(t)$, $g_{p,k}(t)$ and $\delta_k$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H}; Fig.~\ref{fig:model_laser} shows them
for CU2.

\paragraph{Multipass geometry and intensity.} The light crosses the molecular beam 13 times in a
two-prism multipass. The pass centres lie at $s_n=n\times\SI{3.5}{mm}$ ($n=1\ldots13$) along the
molecular beam. Some power is lost at every pass, so the pass that is crossed $p$-th in optical
order carries the power fraction $(1-L)^{p-1}$, where $L$ is the loss per pass. In a two-prism
multipass the optical order of the spatially ordered passes is $p_n=(1,12,3,10,5,8,7,6,9,4,11,2,13)$:
the strongest and the weakest passes alternate. Each pass is an elliptical Gaussian, with
$\sigs=\SI{0.75}{mm}$ along the molecular beam (full $4\sigma$ width \SI{3}{mm}) and $\sigy$ equal
to a quarter of the beam height across it. The passes add incoherently in intensity,
\begin{equation}
  I_k(s,y)=P_k\sum_{n=1}^{13}\frac{(1-L)^{p_n-1}}{2\pi\sigs\sigy}
  \exp\Bigl[-\frac{(s-s_n)^2}{2\sigs^2}-\frac{y^2}{2\sigy^2}\Bigr],
  \label{eq:intensity}
\end{equation}
where $P_k$ is the power of beam $k$ entering the multipass; interference between passes is
neglected. With $L=\SI{5}{\percent}$ the 13 passes carry 9.73 times the power of the first, and with
$L=\SI{10}{\percent}$ 7.46 times. The Rabi rate of the main pair follows from the local intensity,
\begin{equation}
  |\Omega_k(t)|=|m_k|\,d_{XB}\sqrt{\frac{2I_k(vt,y_0)}{c\,\varepsilon_0}},
  \label{eq:rabi}
\end{equation}
for a molecule moving at the forward velocity $v$ at the height $y_0$. The peak values are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:rabi}. All beams drive the main pair below saturation, $\Omega_k<\Gamma$, so a
molecule scatters photons at a rate well below $\Gamma/2$. At \SI{184}{m/s} a molecule crosses the
FWHM of one pass in \SI{9.6}{\micro s} and the whole multipass in \SI{272}{\micro s}.

\paragraph{Polarization switching.} For a fixed polarization, the number of addressed ground
sublevels is larger than the number of excited sublevels they couple to (12 against 10 for CU1; 20
against 14 and 18 for the two CU2 beams, counting both parity parents), so at least the difference is
dark: superpositions of ground
sublevels that the light does not couple to at all. The polarization is therefore switched between X
and Z with a square wave,
\begin{equation}
  g_{Z,0}(t)=\tfrac12\bigl[1+\operatorname{sgn}\sin(2\pi f_{\mathrm p}t)\bigr],\qquad
  g_{X,0}(t)=1-g_{Z,0}(t),
\end{equation}
at $f_{\mathrm p}=\SI{0.5}{MHz}$, i.e.\ about five switching periods per pass
(Fig.~\ref{fig:model_laser}b). A state that is dark for one polarization is in general bright for the
other. The switching is instantaneous and keeps the power constant. In CU2 the two beams pass the
polarization EOM with orthogonal polarizations, so they switch in antiphase:
$g_{Z,1}=g_{X,0}$ and $g_{X,1}=g_{Z,0}$.

\paragraph{Phase modulation.} In CU2 the components driven by each beam spread over about
\SI{90}{MHz}. The laser is therefore phase-modulated by one EOM before the AOM,
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_k(t)\to\Omega_k(t)\,e^{\mathrm i\beta\sin(2\pi f_{\mathrm m}t)},\qquad
  f_{\mathrm m}=\SI{17.5}{MHz},\quad\beta=1.5,
\end{equation}
with the same modulation, including its phase, on both beams. This puts sidebands at
$n\times\SI{17.5}{MHz}$ with power fractions $J_n(\beta)^2$: 0.262 in the carrier, 0.311 in each
first-order sideband, 0.054 in each second-order and 0.004 in each third-order sideband
(Fig.~\ref{fig:model_laser}c). CU1 uses no phase modulation.

\paragraph{Doppler shift.} In the two-prism multipass the light reverses its direction after every
crossing. Because the optical order $p_n$ alternates between odd and even numbers, the direction of
the light also alternates from one spatial pass to the next. A molecule with velocity $v_x$ along
the light therefore sees the detuning
\begin{equation}
  \delta_k\to\delta_k+\sigma(vt)\,k_Lv_x,\qquad \sigma(s)=\pm1,
  \label{eq:doppler}
\end{equation}
where $k_Lv_x/2\pi=\SI{3.68}{MHz}$ per m/s at \SI{271.7}{nm} and $\sigma$ changes sign half-way
between two passes, where the intensity is negligible (shading in Fig.~\ref{fig:model_laser}a). The
molecule thus sees the laser at $\delta+k_Lv_x$ in half of the passes and at $\delta-k_Lv_x$ in the
other half. No laser frequency compensates both, which is why the transverse velocity matters far
more than a common frequency offset of the same size. In CU2 both beams share the multipass and see
the same shift.

\paragraph{AOM.} The two CU2 beams differ in frequency by the AOM frequency, Eq.~\eqref{eq:aom}. In
the model this fixes the detuning of beam 1, $\delta_1=\delta_0+2\pi[\fAOM-\Dmain(E)]$. The two powers
are set independently.

\subsection{Initial states and observables}
\label{sec:model_obs}

\paragraph{Trajectory.} The molecule moves at the forward velocity $v$ through \SI{5}{cm}, from
$s=0$ to $s=\SI{5}{cm}$, with a fixed transverse velocity $v_x$ and a fixed height $y_0$ in the beam.
Over the \SI{272}{\micro s} a molecule with $|v_x|\leq\SI{1}{m/s}$ moves transversely by less than
\SI{0.3}{mm}, small against the beam height. The forward velocity is \SI{184}{m/s} for the set-point
studies and the value measured in each test otherwise (Table~\ref{tab:stages}); no average over the
forward-velocity spread is taken.

\paragraph{Initial states.} The molecules enter with incoherent populations,
$\rho(0)=\sum_i w_i|i\rangle\langle i|$, over a set of sublevels $i$ of the addressed level, selected
by their labels. Four sets are used:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item \emph{uniform}: all sublevels with equal weight, the case without any earlier state selection;
  \item CU1, \emph{SPB-related levels}: the eight $J=1$ sublevels whose $|m_J|=1$ content in the
        uncoupled basis exceeds one half. SPB transfers the molecules into these levels, so $J=1$
        molecules left there cannot be told apart from the transferred ones;
  \item CU1 test, \emph{AP1 levels}: the four mainly-$m_J=0$ sublevels of $J=1$ that AP1 populates
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:cu1_test});
  \item CU2, \emph{SPB source levels}: the four $m_J\approx0$ sublevels of $J=2$ that the lens focuses,
        $|F_1=\tfrac52,F=3,m_F=0,\pm1\rangle$ and $|F_1=\tfrac52,F=2,m_F=0\rangle$.
\end{itemize}
The master equation is linear in $\rho(0)$, so one trajectory per starting sublevel gives the result
for any incoherent mixture, and the residual of each sublevel can be studied on its own
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cu2_two}).

\paragraph{Cleanup efficiency.} The figure of merit is the fraction of the molecules removed from the
addressed level $J$,
\begin{equation}
  \etac(v_x,y_0)=1-\sum_{i\in J}\rho_{ii}(T),
\end{equation}
with the sum over all sublevels of $J$ and $T$ the time at the end of the \SI{5}{cm}. Results quoted
\emph{on the axis} are for $v_x=0$, $y_0=0$.

\paragraph{Mean in the acceptance.} To compare with an experiment, $\etac$ is averaged over the
transverse distribution of the molecules that reach the detection laser,
\begin{equation}
  \bar\eta_{\mathrm c}=\frac{\iint_A G(v_x)\,Y(y_0)\,\etac(v_x,y_0)\,\mathrm dv_x\,\mathrm dy_0}
  {\iint_A G(v_x)\,Y(y_0)\,\mathrm dv_x\,\mathrm dy_0},
  \label{eq:accmean}
\end{equation}
where $G$ and $Y$ are Gaussians with $\sigma_{v_x}=\SI{0.65}{m/s}$ and $\sigy=\SI{10.95}{mm}$ (CU1) or
\SI{10.35}{mm} (CU2), from a simulation of the lensed $J=2$ beam at \SI{25}{kV}, and $A$ is the
detection acceptance, $|v_x|\leq\SI{1}{m/s}$ and $|y_0|\leq\SI{8.5}{mm}$. The integral is evaluated on
a grid of trajectories: for CU1, 21 values of $v_x$ from $-1$ to \SI{1}{m/s} and 11 values of $y_0$ from
0 to \SI{15}{mm}; for CU2, which is about 80 times more expensive, 24 trajectories,
$v_x=\pm0.2,\pm0.6,\pm\SI{1.0}{m/s}$ and $y_0=0$, 3, 6 and \SI{9}{mm}, which agree with a
44-trajectory grid to \SI{0.08}{\percent}. Because the remaining fraction changes roughly
exponentially with the intensity, it is interpolated logarithmically in $y_0$. For the set-point grids
of CU2 even 24 trajectories per point are too many. Each grid point is then scored with a
six-trajectory proxy, $v_x=0,\pm\SI{0.6}{m/s}$ with Gaussian weights and $y_0=0$ and \SI{6}{mm} with
equal weights, and the best points are recomputed on the full grid.

\subsection{Numerics}
\label{sec:model_num}

The master equation is integrated with the adaptive Dormand--Prince method~\cite{Dormand1980} of the
Rust backend of \code{centrex-tlf}, starting from a \SI{2}{ns} step. The relative and absolute
tolerances are $10^{-7}$ and $10^{-9}$. The CU2 scans use $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-8}$, which changed the
CU2 optimum at \SI{200}{V/cm} by only $3\times10^{-5}$. A CU1 trajectory takes about \SI{0.5}{s} in a
scan and a CU2 trajectory about \SI{40}{s}: the CU2 model has 56 instead of 26 levels, and the
integrator has to follow both the \SI{209}{MHz} phase factor of the second beam and the
\SI{17.5}{MHz} modulation. Detunings, powers, $v_x$, $y_0$ and the modulation and switching settings
are runtime parameters and are scanned without rebuilding the model; a new field or set of kept
levels needs a new build.

\section{CU1}

\subsection{Scheme and on-axis optimum}

At \SI{150}{V/cm} the five sublevels of the excited level $B$, $J'=2$, $F_1'=5/2$, $F'=2$, $P'=+$
form three groups: $m_F'=0$ is the highest, $|m_F'|=1$ lies \SI{13.4}{MHz} below it, and $|m_F'|=2$
a further \SI{18.5}{MHz} below. The 12 sublevels of $J=1$ span only \SI{2.4}{MHz}. The design
study~\cite{CleanupTheory2024} started from an EOM whose sidebands reach the outer groups. However,
its scans of detuning against modulation frequency put the minimum of the remaining population at
zero modulation for every power. A single unmodulated laser near the $|m_F'|=1$ pair, with X/Z
switching, is therefore enough.

On the beam axis ($v_x=0$, $y_0=0$), at \SI{80}{mW} with a \SI{4.8}{cm} high beam, the best
detuning is $+\SI{20.2}{MHz}$. There CU1 removes \SI{99.963}{\percent} of the $J=1$ population, and
the removed molecules go to $J=3$ and $J=2$ in about equal parts. The optimum lies close to the
strength-weighted centre of the transition components, $+\SI{19.5}{MHz}$, and does not depend on
the power. It is, however, narrow: the cleanup exceeds \SI{99.9}{\percent} only between $+20.0$ and
$+\SI{20.5}{MHz}$, and \SI{99}{\percent} only within \SI{2.1}{MHz}.

\subsection{Transverse velocity and the set point}
\label{sec:cu1_set}

Because the Doppler shift alternates in sign from pass to pass, the narrow optimum makes CU1 very
sensitive to the transverse velocity. At $+\SI{20.2}{MHz}$ on the axis, the cleanup exceeds
\SI{99.9}{\percent} only for $|v_x|\leq\SI{0.2}{m/s}$ and \SI{99}{\percent} only for
$|v_x|\leq\SI{0.4}{m/s}$. It falls to \SI{97.4}{\percent} at \SI{0.5}{m/s} and to \SI{78}{\percent}
at \SI{1}{m/s}. The transverse position matters less: the cleanup is still \SI{99.5}{\percent} at
$y_0=\SI{12}{mm}$.

Averaged over the velocities, however, the on-axis optimum is not the best detuning. The reason is
the ground-state structure in the field: the 12 sublevels of $J=1$ form two groups \SI{2.4}{MHz}
apart. The four mainly-$m_J=0$ levels, which include the main ground state, are resonant with the
driven $|m_F'|=1$ components at $\delta/2\pi\approx+\SI{18.5}{MHz}$. The eight mainly-$|m_J|=1$
levels, into which SPB transfers the molecules (the \emph{SPB-related} levels), are resonant only at
$+20.7$ to $+\SI{20.9}{MHz}$. This group contains the two most weakly coupled levels,
$|F_1=\tfrac32,F=2,m_F=\pm2\rangle$ and $|F_1=\tfrac12,F=0\rangle$ ($|M|^2=0.005$ and 0.003,
against 0.014--0.106 for the others). At $+\SI{19.0}{MHz}$ the laser is 1.7--\SI{1.9}{MHz} below
this group for a molecule at rest, and the two weak levels keep about \SI{9}{\percent} of their
population. A molecule with $|v_x|\approx\SI{0.5}{m/s}$, however, sees the laser \SI{1.84}{MHz}
higher in half of the passes and is emptied completely. Since most molecules move transversely,
$+\SI{19.0}{MHz}$ is better on average (Table~\ref{tab:cu1_pred}), and it was taken as the set
point. A second maximum lies at $+22.0$ to $+\SI{22.5}{MHz}$. A \SI{1.6}{MHz} phase EOM, which
broadens the laser over the Doppler shifts, gains less than \SI{1}{\percent} and was not adopted.

\subsection{Test on the SPB setup}
\label{sec:cu1_test}

\paragraph{Setup and method.} CU1 was tested on the SPB setup between 2026-10-16 and
2026-10-21~\cite{CU1GroupMeeting2026}. The basic idea is to detect the $J=1$ molecules with CU1 on
and off and to take the ratio of the two signals. The conditions were:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{molecules}: $J=1$, prepared by rotational cooling (RC) on P(2) $F_1'=3/2$, $F'=2$ and
        the first adiabatic passage of SPA (AP1). AP1 transfers all four $J=0$ hyperfine sublevels
        to the four mainly-$m_J=0$ levels of $J=1$, the triplet
        $|F_1=\tfrac32,F=2,m_F=0,\pm1\rangle$ and the singlet
        $|F_1=\tfrac32,F=1,m_F=0\rangle$~\cite{Grasdijk2025AP}; RC on $F'=2$ enhances $J=0$, $F=1$,
        so the triplet dominates. The lens ($\pm\SI{24.7}{kV}$) hardly acts on these molecules.
        Forward velocity about \SI{145}{m/s}.
  \item \textbf{CU1}: \SI{2.5}{m} from the cell, \SI{156}{V/cm}, \SI{50}{mW} (about \SI{45}{mW}
        entering the multipass) in a \SI{2}{cm} beam, 13 passes, raised to about 17 during the test.
  \item \textbf{detection}: \SI{3.15}{m} from the cell, R(1) $F_1'=5/2$, $F'=3$, parallel to CU1,
        accepting $|v_x|$ up to about 1.2--\SI{1.7}{m/s}. In the final experiment the detection is
        \SI{8}{m} downstream behind a \SI{2}{cm} aperture and accepts only
        $|v_x|\leq\SI{0.22}{m/s}$.
  \item \textbf{shutter}: in the CU1 beam, open and closed on consecutive ablation shots, in bursts
        of 10 open and 10 closed shots on one target spot.
\end{itemize}
The detection line couples only the five $F_1=\tfrac32$, $F=2$ sublevels and has no dark state. As
long as every $F=2$ molecule is pumped out during the detection, the ratio of two signals is
therefore the ratio of the $F=2$ populations. Because the shutter alternates on every shot, source
and absorption drifts cancel in the remaining fraction $r=A_{\mathrm{open}}/A_{\mathrm{closed}}$, the
ratio of the fitted fluorescence amplitudes. The efficiency is $\etac=1-r$, with uncertainties from a
bootstrap over bursts.

\paragraph{Progress and final result.} Within two days the remaining fraction fell from 0.099 to
0.026 (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu1_progress}). Most of the gain came from the alignment of the multipass. A
cross alignment laser put the CU1 beam, the detection beam and the chamber centre at the same
height; the number of passes was raised from 13 to about 17; and the reflected beam was overlapped
with the incoming beam, so that the laser is perpendicular to the molecular beam. RC was also moved
from $F'=1$ to $F'=2$. With $F'=1$ the singlet dominates, and CU1 pumps part of it into $F=2$, where
R(1) sees it; with CU1 closed, R(1) cannot see the singlet, so these molecules add only to the
open-shutter signal. Finally, the set point was moved to the measured optimum, which lies
4--\SI{6}{MHz} (UV) below the predicted $+19$ to $+\SI{20}{MHz}$; the origin of this offset is not
yet understood. The final run, 2000 shots with
alignment C, gave $r=0.026\ [0.024,0.028]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu1_final}), i.e.
\begin{equation*}
  \etac=\SI{97.4(2)}{\percent}.
\end{equation*}

\section{CU2}
\label{sec:cu2}

\subsection{Scheme}

At the CU2 field the sublevels of $B$, $J'=3$, $F_1'=7/2$, $F'=3$ are Stark-split by tens of MHz,
while the 20 sublevels of $J=2$ span only about \SI{1.4}{MHz}. The design
study~\cite{CleanupTheory2024} reached the scheme in three steps. First, one laser on $F'=3$ with a
resonant EOM (about \SI{17}{MHz}, $\beta\approx1.5$) cleans only about \SI{98}{\percent}. The
bottleneck is $|J=2,F_1=\tfrac32,F=1,m_F=0\rangle$: it reaches $F'=3$ only through its admixture of
$F=3$, and only the $m_F'=\pm1$ components through the sidebands, so it keeps about a fifth of its
population. Second, a beam on $F'=4$, about \SI{210}{MHz} higher and made from the same laser by an
AOM, removes this bottleneck with the same total power. Third, one EOM placed before the AOM
modulates both beams, which costs little and is much simpler than one EOM per beam. In the adopted
layout, the undiffracted and first-order outputs of the AOM are recombined with orthogonal
polarizations and pass a common polarization EOM, so the two beams switch between X and Z in
antiphase.

The AOM couples the two detunings. With $\delta_k$ measured from the main resonance of beam $k$,
the AOM frequency is the frequency difference of the two beams,
\begin{equation}
  \fAOM=\Dmain(E)+\frac{\delta_1-\delta_0}{2\pi},
  \label{eq:aom}
\end{equation}
where $\Dmain$ is the separation of the two main resonances, \SI{214.59}{MHz} at the measured CU2 field of
\SI{217}{V/cm}. The laser frequency sets $\delta_0$, and the AOM frequency sets $\delta_1-\delta_0$.
The AOM range, $200\pm\SI{15}{MHz}$, limits $(\delta_1-\delta_0)/2\pi$ to
$-29.59\ldots+\SI{0.41}{MHz}$. The components driven by each beam spread over about \SI{90}{MHz},
which is why both beams are phase-modulated.

\subsection{Set point at \SI{217}{V/cm}}
\label{sec:cu2_opt}

The set point was optimised for the measured field and power: \SI{217}{V/cm}, 32 + \SI{43}{mW}
($F'=3$ + $F'=4$, \SI{75}{mW} in total), \SI{5}{\percent} loss per pass, the \SI{3}{cm} beam, the EOM
at \SI{17.5}{MHz} with $\beta=1.5$, and $v_z=\SI{184}{m/s}$ (\code{centrex-tlf} 0.3.0). The full mean
in acceptance needs 24 trajectories, so each grid point is scored with a six-trajectory proxy
($v_x=0,\pm\SI{0.6}{m/s}$; $y_0=0$, \SI{6}{mm}). Two starts are scored: the SPB source levels, i.e.\
the four $m_J\approx0$ sublevels of $J=2$ that the lens focuses, and a uniformly populated $J=2$
level. A coarse $(\delta_0,\fAOM)$ grid over $\delta_0/2\pi=3$--\SI{16}{MHz} and
$\fAOM=190$--\SI{215}{MHz} has its best points near $\delta_0/2\pi=12.5$--\SI{16}{MHz}. The fine grid
around them (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu2_grid}) peaks at
\begin{equation*}
  \delta_0/2\pi=+\SI{14.5}{MHz},\qquad \fAOM=\SI{209}{MHz}\qquad(\delta_1/2\pi=+\SI{8.91}{MHz})
\end{equation*}
for both starts. At this point the full 24-trajectory mean in acceptance is \SI{99.62}{\percent} for
the SPB source levels and \SI{98.82}{\percent} for a uniform start (Table~\ref{tab:cu2_217}).

\subsection{Where the residual is}
\label{sec:cu2_two}

To find which sublevels limit CU2, each $J=2$ sublevel was started alone and the result averaged
over the acceptance (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu2_perstate}). The four lens levels ($m_J\approx0$) keep
0.1--\SI{0.5}{\percent}, and $F_1=\tfrac52$, $F=2$ keeps about \SI{0.2}{\percent}. Two groups
dominate the rest. The eight $F_1=\tfrac32$ sublevels keep \SI{1.9}{\percent} on average, and the
stretched states $|F_1=\tfrac52,F=3,m_F=\pm3\rangle$ \SI{3.5}{\percent} each. The stretched states
are pumped almost only by the $F'=4$ beam, through components far from the main lines. This explains the gap between the two starts: the lens
does not populate $m_F=\pm3$, so the SPB source levels are cleaned to \SI{99.6}{\percent}, but a
uniform start only to \SI{98.8}{\percent}. For the molecules that actually arrive, the residual
after CU2 is dominated by $F_1=\tfrac32$, into which CU2 itself pumps part of the lens molecules
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cu2_test}).

\subsection{Test on the SPB setup}
\label{sec:cu2_test}

\paragraph{Setup.} CU2 was tested on the SPB setup on 2026-11-07 and
2026-11-08~\cite{CU2GroupMeeting2026}. As for CU1, the basic idea is to compare the detection
signals with CU2 on and off. The conditions were:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{molecules}: the $J=2$ molecules focused by the lens, forward velocity
        \SI{156}{m/s}.
  \item \textbf{CU2}: \SI{217}{V/cm}, $\delta_0/2\pi=+\SI{13}{MHz}$, $\fAOM=\SI{209}{MHz}$,
        20 + \SI{30}{mW}. On the axis this point lies on the same plateau as the set point (SPB
        source levels above \SI{99}{\percent} for $\delta_0/2\pi=8$--\SI{15}{MHz}).
  \item \textbf{detection}: \SI{24}{mW} in a $1.5\times\SI{1.5}{cm}$ beam, on one of four R(2)
        lines (Table~\ref{tab:cu2_test_lines}). The light can only be $\sigma^+$ circularly
        polarised. With the detection field plates off, the region is field-free and the
        quantisation axis is the earth field, vertical and perpendicular to $\mathbf k$; about this
        axis the light is $\tfrac12\pi+\tfrac14\sigma^++\tfrac14\sigma^-$.
  \item \textbf{shutter}: in the CU2 beam, alternating on consecutive shots, 50 bursts of 20 shots
        per line (500 shots with CU2 on and 500 with CU2 off).
\end{itemize}
The ratio $R_l=S^{\mathrm{on}}_l/S^{\mathrm{off}}_l$ of line $l$ comes from a template fit:
the mean open-shutter trace is fitted as $R_l$ times the mean closed-shutter trace plus a linear
baseline, with errors from a bootstrap over bursts (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu2_traces}). During the test the
singlet ratio on line B fell from 0.37, with too little CU2 power and a misaligned multipass, to 0.10
after the realignment, and to 0.006 at the final laser frequency.

\paragraph{A line ratio is not a level efficiency.} Ideally, each detection line would see only one
$J=2$ group, and its on/off ratio would directly be the residual of that group. No such line exists
at $E=0$: each line couples several of the four $J=2$ hyperfine groups $F\equiv(F_1,F)$
(Table~\ref{tab:cu2_test_lines}). Line A sees only
$F_1=\tfrac52$, $F=3$ (the triplet). Line B sees the singlet $F_1=\tfrac52$, $F=2$ and, through a
weaker component, the triplet. Line C sees the triplet and $F_1=\tfrac32$, $F=2$, and line D sees all
four. CU2 cleans the groups differently and pumps part of the lens molecules into $F_1=\tfrac32$, so
the weights of the groups in each signal change between the two shutter states. For example, with
CU2 off the signal on C is \SI{99}{\percent} triplet crosstalk, while with CU2 on \SI{95}{\percent}
of it comes from $F_1=\tfrac32$, $F=2$ molecules pumped in by CU2. The \SI{2.6}{\percent} on C is
therefore not the efficiency of any one level. Instead, the populations of the four groups are solved
from the four line ratios together, with the probe photon yields of each group (below). The result is
checked in two ways: the measured line ratios are compared with those predicted directly by the CU2
OBE, without any solving (Table~\ref{tab:cu2_test_lines}), and the solution is repeated with other
start compositions and with flux drifts (\emph{Assumptions} below).

\paragraph{Solving for the groups.} The signal of line $l$ is $S^x_l=\sum_F\eta^x_{lF}N^x_F$ ($x=\mathrm{off},\mathrm{on}$),
where $N^x_F$ is the number of molecules in group $F$ and $\eta^x_{lF}$ the number of probe photons per
molecule. Before CU2 the lens passes only the lens sublevels $L_F$ ($F=3$, $m_F=0,\pm1$; $F=2$,
$m_F=0$), and $F_1=\tfrac32$ is assumed empty. The composition before CU2 then has only one free
parameter, the triplet share $f_t$, and the ratio $r$ of the CU2-off signals of lines B and A fixes
it:
\begin{equation}
  f_F\equiv\frac{N^{\mathrm{off}}_F}{N^{\mathrm{off}}_{J=2}}=(\overset{\scriptstyle F=3}{\mathstrut f_t},\,\overset{\scriptstyle F=2}{\mathstrut 1-f_t},\,
    \overset{\scriptstyle F=2}{\mathstrut 0},\,\overset{\scriptstyle F=1}{\mathstrut 0}),\qquad
  r\equiv\frac{S^{\mathrm{off}}_B}{S^{\mathrm{off}}_A}=\frac{\sum_F\eta^{\mathrm{off}}_{BF}f_F}{\sum_F\eta^{\mathrm{off}}_{AF}f_F}.
  \label{eq:ft}
\end{equation}
The measured $r=1.56\pm0.17$ (two pairs of adjacent runs, 4--7\,min apart) gives $f_t=0.441$. This
is the only place where signals of different runs are compared. The on/off ratio of each line then
cancels the beam flux and the light collection:
\begin{equation}
  R_l=\frac{S^{\mathrm{on}}_l}{S^{\mathrm{off}}_l}=\frac{\sum_F\eta^{\mathrm{on}}_{lF}\,n_F}{\sum_F\eta^{\mathrm{off}}_{lF}\,f_F},
  \qquad n_F\equiv\frac{N^{\mathrm{on}}_F}{N^{\mathrm{off}}_{J=2}}.
  \label{eq:Rl}
\end{equation}
These are four equations for the four fractions $n_F$ left after CU2, in units of all $J=2$ before
CU2. The yields $\eta^x_{lF}$ are averages of the sublevel yields $\eta_l(F,m_F;v_x)$ of the probe OBE
(\SI{24}{mW}, $\sigma^+$ about $\mathbf k$, $E=0$, $v_z=\SI{156}{m/s}$). With CU2 off, they are
averaged over the lens sublevels and the lens $v_x$ distribution $G(v_x)$,
\begin{equation}
  \eta^{\mathrm{off}}_{lF}=\frac{1}{\#L_F}\sum_{m_F\in L_F}\,\sum_{v_x}G(v_x)\,\eta_l(F,m_F;v_x).
  \label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
With CU2 on, they are weighted with the CU2 OBE residual of every start sublevel, i.e.\ with the
residual's own $m_F$ and $v_x$:
\begin{equation}
  \eta^{\mathrm{on}}_{lF}=\frac{\sum_{i\in L}w_i\sum_{v_x,y_0}W(v_x,y_0)\sum_{m_F\in F}P_{i;F,m_F}(v_x,y_0)\,
  \eta_l(F,m_F;v_x)}{\sum_{i\in L}w_i\sum_{v_x,y_0}W(v_x,y_0)\sum_{m_F\in F}P_{i;F,m_F}(v_x,y_0)}.
  \label{eq:etaon}
\end{equation}
Here
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt]
  \item $\#L_F$ is the number of lens sublevels of $F$;
  \item $W(v_x,y_0)$ is $G(v_x)$ times the $y_0$ weight, so that $\sum_{y_0}W=G$;
  \item $L=L_{F=3}\cup L_{F=2}$ are the four start sublevels, with weights $w_i=f_{F(i)}/\#L_{F(i)}$;
  \item $P_{i;F,m_F}(v_x,y_0)$ is the population in $(F,m_F)$ after CU2 of a molecule started in $i$,
        from the CU2 OBE at the test settings and $v_z=\SI{156}{m/s}$. The sum over $m_F$ runs over
        all $2F+1$ sublevels, because CU2 redistributes $m_F$.
\end{itemize}
With CU2 off every molecule stays in its start sublevel, so $\eta^{\mathrm{off}}$ is
$\eta^{\mathrm{on}}$ with $P_{i;F,m_F}\to\delta_{i,(F,m_F)}$. The molecules left by CU2 sit at large $|v_x|$, off the
probe resonance, so $\eta^{\mathrm{on}}<\eta^{\mathrm{off}}$ for the crosstalk terms
(Table~\ref{tab:cu2_test_lines}); with the lens $v_x$ distribution instead, the total residual would
come out \SI{0.2}{\percent} lower. The errors are from a Monte Carlo over $R_l$ and $r$.

Since $\eta$ is nearly lower-triangular, each line fixes essentially one group: A the triplet, B the
singlet, and C and D the two $F_1=\tfrac32$ groups. Adding a line changes the triplet residual by
less than $4\times10^{-5}$ and the other groups by at most $3\times10^{-4}$ of $J=2$, far below their
errors. The line combinations are therefore not independent cross-checks.

\paragraph{Result.} The four lines give the residuals of Table~\ref{tab:cu2_test_res} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:cu2_test_res}. In total \SI{1.07(61)}{\percent} of $J=2$ is left (below \SI{1.85}{\percent}
at 90\,\% CL), i.e.
\begin{equation*}
  \etac=\SI{98.9(6)}{\percent},
\end{equation*}
against \SI{99.3}{\percent} from the CU2 OBE. Every group, and the line ratios predicted directly by
the OBE (Table~\ref{tab:cu2_test_lines}), agree with the model within $1\sigma$. The lens levels
themselves are cleaned to \SI{99.81(62)}{\percent} (triplet) and \SI{99.31(55)}{\percent} (singlet).
Half of what is left is in $F_1=\tfrac32$, $F=2$, pumped in by CU2: in the model, CU2 moves
\SI{0.76}{\percent} of the singlet and \SI{0.27}{\percent} of the triplet into $F_1=\tfrac32$.

\paragraph{Assumptions.} The result depends little on the assumed start composition. Varying $f_t$
from 0.30 to 0.60 changes the total by less than \SI{0.01}{\percent} and leaves the triplet
efficiency at \SI{99.81}{\percent}; only the split between the singlet and $F_1=\tfrac32$, $F=2$
moves. If 0--\SI{5}{\percent} of the $J=2$ population were already in $F_1=\tfrac32$ before CU2, the
total would grow by at most \SI{0.04}{\percent}; these four lines cannot distinguish such molecules
from those pumped in. Drifts of the molecular flux cancel in each $R_l$, because the shutter
alternates on every shot, and enter only through $r$: a \SI{20}{\percent} drift between the A and B
runs moves $f_t$ to 0.38--0.52, inside the range above. The probe power (15 instead of \SI{24}{mW})
and the $m_F$ distribution of the residual change the result by less than its statistical error.

\paragraph{An unexplained discrepancy.} With CU2 off, the signals of C and D relative to A are
$0.36\pm0.06$ and $0.42\pm0.07$, while the model gives 0.59 and 0.70: the measured ratios are about
\SI{40}{\percent} lower. Two causes were considered.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Flux drift.} These runs are 8--\SI{33}{min} apart, much longer than the A and B pair
        (4--7\,min), so a drift of the molecular flux may contribute and cannot be excluded.
  \item \textbf{Probe yields of the weak components.} Without CU2, lines C and D see the lens
        molecules only through weak crosstalk components. The main components saturate at
        1--\SI{2}{mW}, but the weak ones are not yet saturated at \SI{24}{mW}, so their yields
        depend on the intensity that the molecules actually see. The probe OBE at an effective
        \SI{7.5}{mW} gives 0.34 and 0.43, in agreement with the data. Such a low effective
        intensity has not been checked independently, and the groups have not yet been solved
        again with these yields.
\end{enumerate}
The discrepancy therefore remains unexplained. It matters for the result: if the weak yields are
overestimated, $\eta^{\mathrm{on}}_{C,F=3}$ is too large and the $F_1=\tfrac32$, $F=2$ residual is
underestimated. A direct test is detection on P(2) $F_1'=1/2$, $F'=1$, which sees only
$F_1=\tfrac32$ (0.6--0.8 photons per molecule, at most 0.03 for $F_1=\tfrac52$). It measures the
$F_1=\tfrac32$ residual without crosstalk and checks that $F_1=\tfrac32$ is empty before CU2. At the
model residual, its CU2-on signal is about $0.7\sigma$ per 500-shot run, so about 17 runs are needed
for $3\sigma$.

CU1 uses one unmodulated laser on R(1) $F_1'=5/2$, $F'=2$ at \SI{150}{V/cm}. On the axis it removes
\SI{99.96}{\percent} of the $J=1$ population, but its optimum is only \SI{0.6}{MHz} wide. Together
with the Doppler shift, which alternates from pass to pass, this makes CU1 sensitive to the
transverse velocity. The set point, $+\SI{19.0}{MHz}$, uses the Doppler shift to reach the SPB-related levels.
In the test on the SPB setup, CU1 removed \SI{97.4(2)}{\percent} of the detected $F=2$ molecules at
\SI{50}{mW}. The slow power dependence and the measured transverse velocities,
$\sigma_{v_x}=\SI{1.28}{m/s}$, show that the residual comes from molecules with
$|v_x|>\SI{1}{m/s}$, which the test detection accepts. With the measured distribution the model
reproduces the data, and it predicts more than \SI{99.9}{\percent} within the acceptance of the final
experiment.

CU2 uses two beams from one laser, made by an AOM, on $F'=3$ and $F'=4$ of R(2) $F_1'=7/2$. At the measured field of \SI{217}{V/cm}, with
32 + \SI{43}{mW} and \SI{5}{\percent} loss per pass, the laser and AOM frequencies were optimised
together. The set point, $\delta_0/2\pi=+\SI{14.5}{MHz}$ and $\fAOM=\SI{209}{MHz}$, removes
\SI{99.6}{\percent} of the SPB source levels and \SI{98.8}{\percent} of a uniformly populated $J=2$
level within the acceptance. It is flat in $\fAOM$ but narrow in $\delta_0$. The residual is in the
$F_1=\tfrac32$ levels and in the stretched $F=3$, $m_F=\pm3$ sublevels, which the lens does not
populate. In the first test on the SPB setup, CU2 removed \SI{98.9(6)}{\percent} of the $J=2$
population, solved group by group from four detection lines, in agreement with the model
(\SI{99.3}{\percent}). About half of the residual is in $F_1=\tfrac32$, $F=2$, pumped in by CU2
itself.

Both tests differ from the final experiment in ways that these results do not cover. The CU1 test
cleaned molecules from AP1, which populate the four mainly-$m_J=0$ levels of $J=1$, not the
SPB-related levels that SPB will populate, and its detection accepted $|v_x|$ up to
1.2--\SI{1.7}{m/s} instead of \SI{0.22}{m/s}; the offset of its measured optimum from the prediction
is not understood. The CU2 test started only from the lens levels, so neither the stretched
$m_F=\pm3$ sublevels nor the molecules that CU1 pumps into $J=2$ were tested. It ran at 20 +
\SI{30}{mW} instead of 32 + \SI{43}{mW}, and its set point was optimised for
$v_z=\SI{184}{m/s}$ while the molecules moved at \SI{156}{m/s}. With the detection plates off, its
result also relies on the probe yields of the $E=0$ model, and the assumption that $F_1=\tfrac32$ is
empty before CU2 is untested.

Next steps:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item CU1: test the velocity interpretation with a narrower detection acceptance; find the origin of
        the 4--\SI{6}{MHz} offset of the measured optimum; and recompute the set point with the measured
        inputs ($\sigma_{v_x}=\SI{1.28}{m/s}$, 145--\SI{155}{m/s}, 17 passes).
  \item CU2: detect on P(2) $F_1'=1/2$, $F'=1$, which sees only $F_1=\tfrac32$, to measure the residual
        pumped in by CU2 directly and to check that $F_1=\tfrac32$ is empty before CU2; resolve the
        over-predicted CU2-off signals of the weak detection lines; and re-optimise the set point for the
        measured $v_z=\SI{156}{m/s}$.
  \item Both: take the transverse distribution of the molecules that reach the detection laser from
        the beam simulation, separately for the $J=1$ molecules, which the lens does not focus.
\end{enumerate}
