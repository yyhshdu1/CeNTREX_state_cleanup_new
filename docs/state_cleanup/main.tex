\chapter{Rotational-state cleanup}
\label{ch:cleanup}

After the electrostatic lens the CeNTREX molecules are in $X(v{=}0)$, $J=2$, and the
state-preparation stage SPB transfers them to $J=1$, the rotational level used in the
interaction region~\cite{Grasdijk2021}. Two cleanup stages bracket this transfer. CU1, before
SPB, removes the molecules that are already in $J=1$, which would otherwise reach the interaction
region together with the transferred ones. CU2, after SPB, removes the population left in $J=2$.
Both stages pump the molecules optically through the $B\,{}^3\Pi_1$ state into other rotational
levels, in the electric field that is kept on to preserve the spin. This chapter describes the two
schemes and their optical-Bloch-equation (OBE) model (Secs.~\ref{sec:pumping}
and~\ref{sec:model}), the CU1 simulation and a first test of CU1 on the SPB setup
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cu1}), and the optimisation of CU2 at its present operating field
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cu2}).

% =====================================================================
\section{Optical pumping in an electric field}
\label{sec:pumping}

A cleanup stage drives a transition from the addressed rotational level $J$ of $X$ to $B$. Each
excitation is followed by spontaneous decay, and since $B$ decays to several rotational levels,
most decays end in a level that the light does not address: from $B$, $J'=2$, $F'=2$ only
\SI{37}{\percent} of the decays return to $J=1$, and from $J'=3$, $F'=3$ or $F'=4$ only
\SI{27}{\percent} return to $J=2$ (Table~\ref{tab:stages}). A molecule is therefore removed
after one or two photons, provided that every sublevel of the addressed level is excited and none
is left in a dark superposition. States are labelled $|J,F_1,F,m_F,P\rangle$, with the nuclear
spins coupled as $\mathbf J+\mathbf I_1=\mathbf F_1$ and $\mathbf F_1+\mathbf I_2=\mathbf F$;
primed quantum numbers refer to $B$, and R($J$) denotes $J\to J'=J+1$. The level $J=1$ has 12
sublevels ($F_1=\tfrac12$: $F=0,1$; $F_1=\tfrac32$: $F=1,2$), $J=2$ has 20.

The field, \SI{150}{V/cm} for CU1 and \SI{200}{V/cm} for CU2, defines the quantisation axis $z$;
$m_F$ remains a good quantum number, while $J$, $F_1$, $F$ and the parity of $B$ are mixed. The
hyperfine structure of each ground rotational level spans only about \SI{2}{MHz}, but in $B$ the
Stark interaction splits the $|m_F'|$ sublevels of one hyperfine level by 10--\SI{25}{MHz} each,
many natural linewidths ($\Gamma/2\pi=\SI{1.61}{MHz}$). A single laser frequency then reaches
only a few excited sublevels, and for a fixed polarization $N_g$ ground sublevels coupled to
$N_e<N_g$ excited ones leave at least $N_g-N_e$ superpositions dark. Both schemes use three tools
against this:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{reach enough excited sublevels}, with the carrier placed between them, with
        phase-modulation sidebands, or with a second beam on a second hyperfine level;
  \item \textbf{switch the polarization} between X ($\perp\mathbf E$) and Z ($\parallel\mathbf E$)
        at \SI{0.5}{MHz}, so that a superposition dark for one is bright for the other;
  \item \textbf{use field mixing}, which makes zero-field-forbidden transitions driveable. For
        example, $|J=1,F_1=\tfrac12,F=0\rangle$ cannot be excited to $F'=2$ at zero field, but at
        \SI{150}{V/cm} it carries a \SI{29}{\percent} admixture of $|F_1=\tfrac32,F=2\rangle$,
        through which CU1 pumps it.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{States.} The $X$ and $B$ Hamiltonians of \code{centrex-tlf}~\cite{centrextlf}
(rotation with centrifugal distortion, hyperfine and nuclear spin--rotation interactions,
$\Lambda$-doubling of $B$, Stark and Zeeman interactions) are diagonalised in the applied field,
with a magnetic field of \SI{1e-3}{\gauss} along $z$ that lifts the degeneracy of the $\pm m_F$
pairs. Each eigenvector is labelled by the bare state with the largest overlap; at 150 and
\SI{200}{V/cm} this agrees with adiabatic continuation from zero field. The OBE system keeps all
sublevels of the addressed ground level and all $m_F'$ of the addressed excited hyperfine levels,
with all coherences; for CU2 both $\Lambda$-doublet components are kept, because the field mixes
them strongly (for CU1 the partner changes the cleanup by $6\times10^{-6}$). Every other
rotational level of $X$ reachable by decay is compacted into one level, which is exact for the
cleanup because no light acts on it (Table~\ref{tab:stages}).

\paragraph{Hamiltonian and decay.} In the rotating-wave approximation, with $\hbar=1$,
\begin{equation}
  H(t)=H_0-\sum_k\delta_k\Pi_k
  +\frac12\sum_k\Bigl[\Omega_k(t)\sum_{p\in\{X,Z\}}g_{p,k}(t)\,\frac{M^{(p)}_k}{m_k}+\mathrm{h.c.}\Bigr],
  \label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
where $H_0$ holds the field-dressed energies, $\Pi_k$ projects onto the excited states of beam $k$,
$M^{(p)}_k$ is the dipole matrix between the field-dressed states for polarization $p$, normalised
by the main-pair element $m_k$, and $g_{p,k}(t)$ is the polarization gate. Whether a pair is coupled
is decided by this mixed-state matrix element, not by the zero-field selection rules. The detuning
$\delta_k$ is measured from the main resonance of beam $k$. Phase modulation enters as
$\Omega_k\to\Omega_k e^{\mathrm i\beta\sin\omega_{\mathrm m}t}$; for CU2 both beams are written in
the rotating frame of beam 0, so the couplings of beam 1 carry $e^{2\pi\mathrm i\fAOM t}$. The
excited states decay at $\Gamma=1/\tau=\SI{1.01e7}{s^{-1}}$ ($\tau=\SI{99}{ns}$), with branching
ratios computed between the dressed states into every $X$ level, and
\begin{equation}
  \dot\rho=-\mathrm i[H,\rho]+\sum_{e,g}\Bigl(C_{eg}\rho C_{eg}^\dagger-\tfrac12\{C_{eg}^\dagger
  C_{eg},\rho\}\Bigr),\qquad C_{eg}=\sqrt{\Gamma b_{e\to g}}\,|g\rangle\langle e|.
  \label{eq:lindblad}
\end{equation}
With one collapse operator per pair, decay transfers populations but no ground-state coherences.
The system is closed, $\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho=1$ to $10^{-14}$. Decay to $X(v\geq1)$ is not modelled,
which can only underestimate the cleanup.

\paragraph{Laser and trajectory.} The light crosses the molecular beam 13 times in a two-prism
multipass. The passes are Gaussian, with $\sigs=\SI{0.75}{mm}$ along the beam and $\sigy$ equal to a
quarter of the beam height across it. The pass crossed $p$-th in optical order carries the power
fraction $(1-L)^{p-1}$, with $L$ the loss per pass; the passes add incoherently in intensity, and
$|\Omega_k|\propto\sqrt{I_k}$. In the laser frame
$z$ is along the field (vertical in the laboratory) and $x$ is horizontal along the molecular beam;
the light propagates horizontally across it. Consecutive passes counter-propagate, so a molecule
with transverse velocity $v_x$ along the light sees $\delta+k_Lv_x$ in half of the passes and
$\delta-k_Lv_x$ in the others ($k_Lv_x/2\pi=\SI{3.68}{MHz}$ per m/s at \SI{271.7}{nm}): no laser
frequency compensates both. One molecule is followed through \SI{5}{cm} at the mean forward
velocity, with fixed $v_x$ and fixed offset $y_0$ along the beam height. The figure of merit is the
cleanup efficiency $\etac=1-\sum_{i\in J}\rho_{ii}(T)$. Grids over $(v_x,y_0)$ are averaged with the
Gaussian distribution of a simulation of the lensed beam within the acceptance of the detection
laser (Table~\ref{tab:stages}); this \emph{mean in acceptance} is the number quoted for the
transverse distribution. Since the master equation is linear in $\rho(0)$, one trajectory per
starting sublevel gives the result for any incoherent initial mixture.

\paragraph{Numerics.} The master equation is integrated with the adaptive Dormand--Prince
method~\cite{Dormand1980} of the Rust backend of \code{centrex-tlf} (versions 0.2.5 and 0.3.0, which
agree to $10^{-6}$ for CU1), with relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-7}$ and $10^{-9}$ ($10^{-6}$
and $10^{-8}$ for the CU2 scans, which changes the CU2 optimum by $3\times10^{-5}$). A CU1 trajectory
costs about \SI{0.5}{s} in a scan, a CU2 trajectory about \SI{40}{s}, because of the second beam's
\SI{200}{MHz} phase factor and the \SI{17.5}{MHz} modulation. Scans are stored with their parameters
in HDF5 files (\code{results/}); the scripts are in \code{scripts/}.

\section{CU1}
\label{sec:cu1}

\subsection{Scheme and on-axis optimum}

At \SI{150}{V/cm} the five sublevels of $B$, $J'=2$, $F_1'=5/2$, $F'=2$, $P'=+$ form three
groups: $m_F'=0$ highest, $|m_F'|=1$ \SI{17.4}{MHz} below and $|m_F'|=2$ a further
\SI{24.4}{MHz} below; the 12 sublevels of $J=1$ span \SI{2.4}{MHz}. The design
study~\cite{CleanupTheory2024} started from an EOM whose sidebands reach the outer groups, but its
scans of detuning against modulation frequency put the minimum of the remaining population at
zero modulation for every power. A single unmodulated laser near the $|m_F'|=1$ pair, with X/Z
switching, is enough.

On the beam axis ($v_x=0$, $y_0=0$), at \SI{80}{mW} with a \SI{4.8}{cm} high beam, the best
detuning is $+\SI{20.2}{MHz}$, where CU1 removes \SI{99.963}{\percent} of the $J=1$ population;
the removed molecules go to $J=3$ and $J=2$ in about equal parts. The optimum coincides with the
strength-weighted centre of the transition components, $+\SI{19.5}{MHz}$, and does not depend on
the power, but it is narrow: the cleanup exceeds \SI{99.9}{\percent} only between $+20.0$ and
$+\SI{20.5}{MHz}$, and \SI{99}{\percent} within \SI{2.1}{MHz}.

\subsection{Transverse velocity and the set point}
\label{sec:cu1_set}

Because the Doppler shift alternates from pass to pass, the narrow optimum makes CU1 very sensitive
to the transverse velocity. At $+\SI{20.2}{MHz}$ on the axis the cleanup exceeds
\SI{99.9}{\percent} only for $|v_x|\leq\SI{0.2}{m/s}$ and \SI{99}{\percent} only for
$|v_x|\leq\SI{0.4}{m/s}$; it falls to \SI{97.4}{\percent} at \SI{0.5}{m/s} and to \SI{78}{\percent}
at \SI{1}{m/s} (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu1_vx}). The transverse position matters less (\SI{99.5}{\percent}
at $y_0=\SI{12}{mm}$).

Averaged over the velocities, the on-axis optimum is not the best detuning, because of the
ground-state structure in the field. The 12 sublevels of $J=1$ form two groups \SI{2.4}{MHz} apart.
The four mainly-$m_J=0$ levels, which include the main ground state, reach the driven $|m_F'|=1$
components at $\delta/2\pi\approx+\SI{18.5}{MHz}$. The eight mainly-$|m_J|=1$ levels, into which
SPB transfers the molecules (the \emph{SPB-related} levels), reach them only at $+20.7$ to
$+\SI{20.9}{MHz}$. This group contains the two most weakly coupled levels,
$|F_1=\tfrac32,F=2,m_F=\pm2\rangle$ and $|F_1=\tfrac12,F=0\rangle$ ($|M|^2=0.005$ and 0.003,
against 0.014--0.106 for the others). At $+\SI{19.0}{MHz}$ the laser is 1.7--\SI{1.9}{MHz} below
this group for a molecule at rest, and the two weak levels keep about \SI{9}{\percent} of their
population; a molecule with $|v_x|\approx\SI{0.5}{m/s}$, however, sees the laser \SI{1.84}{MHz}
higher in half of the passes and is emptied completely. Since most molecules move transversely,
$+\SI{19.0}{MHz}$ is better on average (Table~\ref{tab:cu1_pred}), and it was taken as the set point.
A second maximum lies at $+22.0$ to $+\SI{22.5}{MHz}$. A \SI{1.6}{MHz} phase EOM that broadens the
laser over the Doppler shifts gains less than \SI{1}{\percent} and was not adopted.

The test of Sec.~\ref{sec:cu1_test} used \SI{50}{mW} in a \SI{2}{cm} beam (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu1_map}).
The best detuning is again $+\SI{19.0}{MHz}$, but the mean in acceptance drops from 98.7 to
\SI{96.5}{\percent}: the peak intensity is similar to that of \SI{80}{mW} in \SI{3}{cm}, but at
$y_0=\SI{8.5}{mm}$ the \SI{2}{cm} beam has only \SI{24}{\percent} of its peak intensity, against
\SI{53}{\percent}. The test molecules come from AP1, which populates the four mainly-$m_J=0$ levels

4--\SI{5}{\percent} at \SI{3}{mrad}: the laser must be perpendicular to the molecular beam within
about \SI{1}{mrad}.

\subsection{Test on the SPB setup}
\label{sec:cu1_test}

\paragraph{Setup and method.} CU1 was tested on the SPB setup between 2026-10-16 and
2026-10-21~\cite{CU1GroupMeeting2026}. Rotational cooling (RC) and the first adiabatic passage of
SPA (AP1) prepare the $J=1$ molecules. AP1 transfers all four $J=0$ hyperfine sublevels to the four
mainly-$m_J=0$ levels of $J=1$, the triplet $|F_1=\tfrac32,F=2,m_F=0,\pm1\rangle$ and the singlet
$|F_1=\tfrac32,F=1,m_F=0\rangle$~\cite{Grasdijk2025AP}. RC on P(2) $F_1'=3/2$, $F'=2$ enhances
$J=0$, $F=1$, so the triplet dominates. The lens ($\pm\SI{24.7}{kV}$) hardly acts on these molecules.
CU1 is \SI{2.5}{m} from the cell, at \SI{156}{V/cm}, with \SI{50}{mW} (about \SI{45}{mW} entering the
multipass) in a \SI{2}{cm} beam. The detection laser, \SI{3.15}{m} from the cell, drives R(1)
$F_1'=5/2$, $F'=3$. This line couples only the five $F_1=\tfrac32$, $F=2$ sublevels and has no dark
state, so as long as every $F=2$ molecule is pumped out, the ratio of two signals is the ratio of the
$F=2$ populations. It propagates parallel to CU1 and accepts $|v_x|$ up to about 1.2--\SI{1.7}{m/s};
in the final experiment the detection, \SI{8}{m} downstream behind a \SI{2}{cm} aperture, accepts
only $|v_x|\leq\SI{0.22}{m/s}$. A shutter in the CU1 beam alternates between open and closed on
consecutive ablation shots, in bursts of 10 open and 10 closed shots on one target spot, so that
source and absorption drifts cancel in the remaining fraction $r=A_{\mathrm{open}}/A_{\mathrm{closed}}$,
the ratio of the fitted fluorescence amplitudes. The efficiency is $\etac=1-r$, with uncertainties
from a bootstrap over bursts.

\paragraph{Progress and final result.} The remaining fraction fell from 0.099 to 0.026 within two
days (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu1_progress}). The main steps were the alignment of the multipass: a cross
alignment laser put the CU1 beam, the detection beam and the chamber centre at the same height; the
number of passes was raised from 13 to about 17; and the reflected beam was overlapped with the
incoming beam, so that the laser is perpendicular to the molecular beam. RC was moved from $F'=1$ to
$F'=2$: with $F'=1$ the singlet dominates, and CU1 pumps part of it into $F=2$, where R(1) sees it,
while R(1) cannot see the singlet with CU1 closed. The set point was moved to the measured optimum,
which lies 4--\SI{6}{MHz} (UV) below the predicted $+19$ to $+\SI{20}{MHz}$. The final run, 2000
shots with alignment C, gave $r=0.026\ [0.024,0.028]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu1_final}), i.e.
\begin{equation*}
  \etac=\SI{97.4(2)}{\percent}.
\end{equation*}

\paragraph{Power dependence and transverse velocity.} The remaining fraction falls only slowly with
the CU1 power $P$: a fit of $r=\exp(-kP^a)$ from 13 to \SI{50}{mW} gives $a=0.45\pm0.03$, whereas a
homogeneous ensemble gives $a=1$. This points to a wide distribution of Doppler shifts. A scan of
the detection laser across R(1) $F'=3$ with CU1 off measures the $v_x$ distribution of the detected
molecules directly (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu1_doppler}): $\sigma_{v_x}=\SI{1.28(3)}{m/s}$, the same at 100 and
\SI{400}{\micro W}, and close to a simulation of the $J=1$ beam through the lens (\SI{1.37}{m/s}).
It is twice the value assumed in Sec.~\ref{sec:cu1_set}, which was that of the lensed $J=2$ beam.

\section{CU2}
\label{sec:cu2}

\subsection{Scheme}

At the original field of \SI{250}{V/cm} the sublevels of $B$, $J'=3$, $F_1'=7/2$, $F'=3$, $P'=-$
are split by about \SI{18}{MHz} per step in $|m_F'|$, and the 20 sublevels of $J=2$ span
\SI{1.4}{MHz}. The design study~\cite{CleanupTheory2024} reached the scheme in three steps. One laser
on $F'=3$ with a resonant EOM (about \SI{17}{MHz}, $\beta\approx1.5$, \SI{100}{mW}) cleans only about
\SI{98}{\percent}: $|J=2,F_1=\tfrac32,F=1,m_F=0\rangle$ reaches $F'=3$ only through its admixture of
$F=3$, and only the $m_F'=\pm1$ components, through the sidebands, so it keeps about a fifth of its
population. A second beam on $F'=4$, about \SI{200}{MHz} higher and made from the same laser by an
AOM, removes this bottleneck with the same total power. Finally, one EOM placed before the AOM
modulates both beams; this costs little (about \SI{99.6}{\percent} at 60 + \SI{18}{mW}, against
\SI{99.95}{\percent} with an EOM on each beam) and is much simpler. In the adopted layout the
undiffracted and first-order outputs of the AOM are recombined with orthogonal polarizations and pass
a common polarization EOM, so the two beams switch between X and Z in antiphase.

The AOM couples the two detunings. With $\delta_k$ measured from the main resonance of beam $k$, the
AOM frequency is the frequency difference of the beams,
\begin{equation}
  \fAOM=\Dmain(E)+\frac{\delta_1-\delta_0}{2\pi},
  \label{eq:aom}
\end{equation}
where $\Dmain$ is the separation of the two main resonances, \SI{211.83}{MHz} at \SI{200}{V/cm} and
\SI{219.93}{MHz} at \SI{250}{V/cm}. The laser frequency sets $\delta_0$ and the AOM frequency
$\delta_1-\delta_0$; the AOM range $200\pm\SI{15}{MHz}$ limits $(\delta_1-\delta_0)/2\pi$ to
$-26.83\ldots+\SI{3.17}{MHz}$ at \SI{200}{V/cm}. The components driven by each beam spread over
about \SI{87}{MHz} ($F'=3$: 0 to $+\SI{87}{MHz}$ from its main resonance; $F'=4$: $-31$ to
$+\SI{56}{MHz}$), which is why both beams are phase-modulated.

\subsection{Optimisation at \SI{200}{V/cm}}
\label{sec:cu2_opt}

CU2 now operates at about \SI{200}{V/cm}. The detunings chosen at \SI{250}{V/cm},
$(\delta_0,\delta_1)/2\pi=(+18.5,-\SI{6.5}{MHz})$, which gave \SI{99.99}{\percent} there, give only
\SI{92.9}{\percent} at \SI{200}{V/cm}: $\Dmain$ has moved by \SI{8.1}{MHz}, and the component
positions within each manifold have moved as well. A scan of the laser frequency at the AOM frequency
that keeps the former detuning difference (\SI{186.83}{MHz}) reaches \SI{99.33}{\percent} at best;
laser and AOM frequency must be optimised together. The two-dimensional scan uses the experimental
coordinates $(\delta_0,\fAOM)$: a coarse \SI{2}{MHz} grid over $\delta_0/2\pi=+5\ldots+\SI{23}{MHz}$,
about one EOM period, and the full AOM range (160 points, Fig.~\ref{fig:cu2_map}); \SI{0.5}{MHz} grids
around its three best local maxima (219 points); and a check of the best points with the tighter
tolerances. Only 15 of the 160 coarse points exceed \SI{99}{\percent}. The fine grids give the optima
of Table~\ref{tab:cu2_opt}: R2 with \SI{99.85}{\percent} at $(+\SI{11.5}{MHz},\SI{191.0}{MHz})$ and,
nearly equivalent, R1 with \SI{99.80}{\percent} at $(+\SI{11.5}{MHz},\SI{208.5}{MHz})$, both well
inside the AOM range.

Both optima are 0.5--\SI{1.5}{MHz} wide at the \SI{99.7}{\percent} level and 2--\SI{3}{MHz} wide at
\SI{99}{\percent} (Table~\ref{tab:cu2_window}). They are elongated along lines of constant $\delta_1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu2_fine}):
$\delta_1$ must be held to
about $\pm\SI{0.5}{MHz}$, whereas $\delta_0$ tolerates $\pm\SI{1.5}{MHz}$ if $\delta_1$ is kept fixed.
A drift of the laser frequency, or of the field, shifts both detunings together and is therefore
limited by the tighter $\delta_1$ tolerance; the field variation of about \SI{2}{V/cm}
(\SI{2}{MHz}) across the cleanup region estimated in the design study is comparable to the
\SI{99}{\percent} width.

\subsection{Two optima and the bottleneck}
\label{sec:cu2_two}

R1 and R2 differ by \SI{17.5}{MHz} in $\fAOM$, exactly one EOM period, with beam 0 unchanged. The comb
of beam 1, at $\delta_1/2\pi+n\times\SI{17.5}{MHz}$, therefore occupies the same frequencies in both
settings, and only the order $n$ of each line differs by one (Fig.~\ref{fig:cu2_scheme}). At
$\beta=1.5$ the carrier carries \SI{26}{\percent} of the power and each first-order sideband
\SI{31}{\percent}. The two lines that matter most sit on the strongest $F'=4$ component, $m_F'=0$ at
$+\SI{8.65}{MHz}$, and on $m_F'=\pm2$ at $-\SI{10.32}{MHz}$; these are \SI{19.0}{MHz} apart, close to
the comb spacing, and receive nearly the same power in both settings (Table~\ref{tab:cu2_comb}). Beam 0 is the same in both: its
first-order sideband sits on the $F'=3$, $m_F'=\pm1$ components and its carrier \SI{3}{MHz} below
$m_F'=\pm2$. Within the AOM range only these two copies of the optimum exist.

\subsection{Transverse motion at the present beam height}
\label{sec:cu2_vx}

In the experiment the CU2 beams are now about \SI{3}{cm} high at the same 80 + \SI{24}{mW}, 0.8 times
the peak intensity of the \SI{2.4}{cm} beam used for the optimisation. The two beams share the
multipass, so the Doppler shift is common to both. Table~\ref{tab:cu2_vx} and Fig.~\ref{fig:cu2_vx}
give R1 and R2 over the transverse velocity and position, for all 20 sublevels and for the four
SPB source levels ($J=2$, $m_J=0$, weighted 2/3 for the singlet and 1/9 for each triplet level).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The transverse position dominates.} The intensity falls to 73, 49 and
        \SI{28}{\percent} of its axial value at $y_0=6$, 9 and \SI{12}{mm}, and from the uniform
        start the cleanup falls from \SI{99.5}{\percent} on the axis to \SI{96.0}{\percent} at
        \SI{9}{mm}.
  \item \textbf{The velocity matters much less than for CU1}: \SI{97.8}{}--\SI{98.4}{\percent} at
        $|v_x|=\SI{0.4}{m/s}$ and \SI{94.7}{}--\SI{95.5}{\percent} at \SI{1}{m/s} on the axis. Each
        beam carries a comb of five significant lines on 32 sublevels, so a shift of a few MHz moves
        some lines off their components while others come closer.
  \item \textbf{R1 is the more robust setting} for both starts and is recommended: within the
        acceptance it removes \SI{96.7}{\percent} of a uniformly populated $J=2$ level and
        \SI{99.6}{\percent} of the SPB source levels.
\end{itemize}
For the $J=2$ molecules that SPB leaves behind, which the lens has focused, the lensed-beam
distribution is the right input; molecules that CU1 pumps into $J=2$ have the wider $J=1$
distribution of Sec.~\ref{sec:cu1_test}. The CU2 results were computed with \code{centrex-tlf}~0.2.5.
Version 0.2.8 restored the Stark mixing with lower $J$ in the $X$ Hamiltonian, which moves $J=2$ line
positions by up to about \SI{1}{MHz}, so the optimum must be re-run, together with a re-optimisation
for the \SI{3}{cm} beam, before it is relied on at the sub-MHz level.

CU1 uses one unmodulated laser on R(1) $F_1'=5/2$, $F'=2$ at \SI{150}{V/cm}. On the axis it removes
\SI{99.96}{\percent} of the $J=1$ population, but its optimum is only \SI{0.6}{MHz} wide, and the
Doppler shift, alternating from pass to pass, makes it sensitive to the transverse velocity. The set
point $+\SI{19.0}{MHz}$ uses the Doppler shift to reach the SPB-related levels. In the test on the SPB
setup CU1 removed \SI{97.4(2)}{\percent} of the detected $F=2$ molecules at \SI{50}{mW}. The slow power
dependence and the measured transverse velocities, $\sigma_{v_x}=\SI{1.28}{m/s}$, show that the residual
comes from $|v_x|>\SI{1}{m/s}$, which the test detection accepts. With the measured distribution the
model reproduces the data, and it predicts more than \SI{99.9}{\percent} for the acceptance of the final
experiment.

CU2 uses two beams from one laser, on $F'=3$ and $F'=4$ of R(2) $F_1'=7/2$, coupled by an AOM and
phase-modulated by one EOM. At \SI{200}{V/cm} laser and AOM frequency had to be optimised together; the
optima, \SI{99.85}{\percent} at $\fAOM=\SI{191.0}{MHz}$ and \SI{99.80}{\percent} at \SI{208.5}{MHz},
lie one EOM period apart and are 2--\SI{3}{MHz} wide at \SI{99}{\percent}. With the present \SI{3}{cm}
beam R1 ($\fAOM=\SI{208.5}{MHz}$) is the more robust setting: \SI{96.7}{\percent} of a uniformly
populated $J=2$ level and \SI{99.6}{\percent} of the SPB source levels within the acceptance, limited by
the beam edges and by the stretched sublevels.

Next steps:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item CU1: test the velocity interpretation with a narrower detection acceptance; find the origin of
        the 4--\SI{6}{MHz} offset of the measured optimum; recompute the set point with the measured
        inputs ($\sigma_{v_x}=\SI{1.28}{m/s}$, 145--\SI{155}{m/s}, 17 passes).
  \item CU2: re-run with \code{centrex-tlf} 0.2.8 or later, re-optimise laser and AOM frequency and the
        power split for the \SI{3}{cm} beam, check the field uniformity at \SI{200}{V/cm}, and test CU2 on
        the setup.
  \item Both: take the transverse distribution of the molecules that reach the detection laser from
        the beam simulation, separately for the $J=1$ molecules, which the lens does not focus.
\end{enumerate}
